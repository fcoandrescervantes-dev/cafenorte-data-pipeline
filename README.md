# CafeNorte Data Pipeline

## Overview

This project implements an end-to-end data pipeline for CafeNorte that integrates data from multiple operational systems into a consistent analytical model.

The pipeline ingests, normalizes, reconciles, validates, and analyzes data from:

- Point-of-sale (POS) transactions
- Ecommerce orders
- Inventory snapshots
- Product and SKU mappings
- Product cost history
- Foreign exchange rates
- Store metadata

The solution produces a unified analytical model that supports business analysis across physical stores and ecommerce while explicitly preserving incomplete or unknown source data instead of silently replacing it with artificial values.

The pipeline is implemented in Python using pandas and PyArrow and includes automated validation with pytest.

---

## Business Questions

The analytical layer addresses four main business questions:

1. **Inventory turnover**  
   Identify the top 10 products by inventory turnover over the latest six-month inventory period.

2. **Stockout events**  
   Identify store-product combinations with stockout periods longer than three consecutive days during the last complete quarter.

3. **Monthly sales growth**  
   Compare monthly revenue and month-over-month growth between the physical and ecommerce channels.

4. **Negative-margin products**  
   Identify products and stores where known historical product costs result in negative margins.

The processing flow is organized into five main stages:

```text

   |
   v

   |
   v
Normalization
   |
   v
Product Reconciliation
   |
   v
Analytical Model
   |
   v
Business Analytics
   |
   v

```

### 1. Ingestion

`src/ingest.py`

Loads the original CSV, Parquet, and JSON sources without applying business transformations.

### 2. Normalization

`src/transform.py`

Standardizes:

- column types
- timestamps
- currencies
- store metadata
- SKU mappings
- inventory snapshots
- historical product costs

### 3. Reconciliation

`src/reconcile.py`

Reconciles the different product identifiers used by the source systems:

```text
POS SKU
   |
   v
Product Dimension
   ^
   |
ERP SKU
   ^
   |
Shopify Handle
```

The reconciliation layer also:

- applies foreign exchange rates
- creates a unified sales fact table
- attaches product attributes
- assigns historical costs
- calculates COGS and margin
- builds the inventory fact table

### 4. Business Analytics

`src/analytics.py`

Calculates the analytical outputs required for the four business questions.

### 5. Orchestration

`src/pipeline.py`

Runs the complete workflow from source ingestion through analytical output generation.

## Analytical Data Model

The pipeline produces an analytical model composed of two dimensions, two fact tables, and one historical cost table.

Provides a unified product identity across the source systems.

- `product_key`

- `sku_erp`
- `shopify_handle`

- `category`

### `fact_inventory`

Contains normalized inventory snapshots at the store-product-date level.

The natural analytical key is:

## Product Reconciliation Strategy

One of the main data-engineering challenges is that the source systems do not use a single universal product identifier.

The three relevant identifiers are:

```text
POS          -> sku_pos
Inventory    -> sku_erp
Ecommerce    -> shopify_handle
```

The pipeline therefore builds a canonical product dimension that connects these identifiers when mappings are available.

A generated analytical key is assigned to every resulting product:

```text
product_key
```

This allows the fact tables to reference a common product identifier even when the operational identifiers differ.

The resulting product dimension contains:

```text
86 analytical products
```

with three mapping states:

### `complete`

The product has all required source identifiers available for cross-system reconciliation.

### `partial`

The product can be identified in more than one context, but one or more operational identifiers are unavailable.

### `unmapped`

The product exists in source activity but cannot be reliably connected to another source-system identifier.

The pipeline intentionally preserves partial and unmapped records rather than dropping their transactions.

This prevents reconciliation problems from silently reducing reported sales or inventory volumes.

## Currency Conversion

Physical POS transactions are already denominated in MXN.

Ecommerce transactions may be denominated in:

```text
MXN
USD
EUR
```

For MXN transactions:

```text
rate_to_mxn = 1
```

For USD and EUR transactions, the pipeline joins the transaction date and currency to the corresponding daily exchange rate.

Revenue is then standardized as:

```text
revenue_mxn = amount_original * rate_to_mxn
```

All foreign-currency ecommerce transactions in the provided dataset have a corresponding exchange rate.

This creates a common MXN revenue measure across both sales channels.

## Historical Cost and Margin Logic

Product costs change over time.

Using the most recent cost for every historical transaction would introduce temporal inaccuracies. The pipeline therefore assigns the cost that was effective for the ERP product at the transaction date.

Conceptually:

```text
For each transaction:

select the latest product cost
where:

effective_date <= transaction_date
```

Once the applicable historical cost is identified:

```text
COGS = quantity * unit_cost_mxn
```

and:

```text
margin_mxn = revenue_mxn - COGS
```

This enables margin analysis using the cost information that was valid at the time of the transaction.

## Missing Data Strategy

A central design principle of the pipeline is:

```text
UNKNOWN != ZERO
```

Missing operational information is therefore preserved instead of being silently replaced with zero.

### Unknown product cost

If a transaction cannot be connected to an ERP product with historical cost information:

```text
unit_cost_mxn = NULL
cogs_mxn      = NULL
margin_mxn    = NULL
```

The pipeline does **not** assign a zero cost because doing so would create an artificial positive margin.

The final sales fact contains:

```text
81,100 transactions with known historical cost
15,337 transactions with unknown historical cost
```

This corresponds to approximately:

```text
84.1% historical cost coverage
```

### Unknown inventory

Inventory follows the same principle.

A value of:

```text
stock_quantity = 0
```

means the system explicitly reported zero inventory and therefore represents a stockout.

A value of:

```text
stock_quantity = NULL
```

means inventory was not known for that observation.

The dataset contains:

```text
4,417 inventory snapshots with unknown stock
```

These observations are explicitly prevented from being classified as stockouts.

## Data Integrity

The pipeline preserves the original transaction volumes during reconciliation.

Final sales volume:

```text
86,490 POS
+9,947 ecommerce
----------------
96,437 total transactions
```

No sales transactions are removed because of incomplete product mappings or unavailable historical costs.

The final model also enforces or validates:

- unique transaction identifiers
- valid product references
- valid physical-store references
- positive sales quantities
- non-negative revenue
- positive exchange rates
- MXN exchange rate equal to 1
- FX conversion consistency
- historical COGS consistency
- margin calculation consistency
- unique inventory natural keys
- valid inventory product references
- valid inventory store references
- non-negative known inventory
- separation of missing inventory from confirmed stockouts

## Key Design Decisions

Several implementation decisions were made to preserve analytical correctness:

- Missing inventory is preserved as unknown rather than converted to zero.
- Stockouts require explicitly known zero inventory.
- Historical product costs are assigned according to the transaction date.
- Transactions without a known historical cost retain a missing cost and margin.
- POS, ERP, and Shopify product identifiers are reconciled through a common product dimension.
- Unmapped source identifiers are preserved instead of dropping their transactions.
- Foreign-currency ecommerce transactions are converted using the corresponding daily exchange rate.
- The physical and ecommerce channels are combined into a common sales fact schema.
- Generated analytical datasets are separated from source code and excluded from Git.
- Automated tests validate both technical integrity and important business rules.
